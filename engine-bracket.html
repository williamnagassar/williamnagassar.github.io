Aircraft Engine Loading Bracket Topology Optimization

MANE 4600U • Ontario Tech University • Individual Project

Executive Summary

This project details the structural analysis and topology optimization of an aircraft engine loading bracket intended for Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) manufacturing. The primary objective was to achieve a 50% reduction in weight while maintaining structural integrity across four distinct flight and operational load cases. The optimization and validation were performed using ANSYS Workbench.

Methodology & Simulation Setup

Software UsedANSYS Workbench
Manufacturing ConstraintFused Deposition Modeling (FDM)
Optimization Target50% Total Weight Reduction
Boundary ConditionsFixed condition applied to all interface mounting holes

Optimization Results

The topology optimization solver was run targeting a 50% mass reduction. Due to the significantly higher maximum stress in Load Case 1, its objective weight was iteratively increased to 5 to ensure the final geometry would not yield.

Final Mass Reduction51.1% (Reduced from 4.52 lbs to 2.31 lbs)
Final Volume Reduction51.1% (Reduced from 28.27 in³ to 14.44 in³)
Minimum Factor of Safety1.14 (Occurs during Load Case 1)

Applied Load Cases & Structural Validation

Four independent static structural load cases were evaluated with forces applied directly to the pin hole geometry. The optimized geometry was validated against these cases to ensure yielding would not occur.

Load Case 1 (Vertical)

Load: 8,000 lbs Upward

Original Max Stress: 99.9 ksi

Optimized Max Stress: 115 ksi

Factor of Safety: 1.14

Load Case 2 (Horizontal)

Load: 8,500 lbs to the Right

Original Max Stress: 62.9 ksi

Optimized Max Stress: 69.8 ksi

Factor of Safety: 1.88

Load Case 3 (Off-Axis)

Load: 9,500 lbs @ 42° relative to vertical

Original Max Stress: 51.0 ksi

Optimized Max Stress: 70.0 ksi

Factor of Safety: 1.87

Load Case 4 (Torsional)

Load: 5,000 lb-in Clockwise Moment

Original Max Stress: 51.6 ksi

Optimized Max Stress: 52.7 ksi

Factor of Safety: 2.48

Conclusion & Recommendations

The topology optimization successfully resulted in a final mass of 2.31 lbs, surpassing the goal with a highly efficient 51.1% weight reduction. Structural validation confirms that the part will not yield under the prescribed load cases. However, for real-world aerospace applications, a minimum factor of safety of around 1.5 is recommended to account for dynamic loading and the inherent material anisotropy of FDM printing. Future iterations should prioritize meeting this structural threshold over a strict mass reduction target.
